SECTION 1: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
================================================================================

1.1 CORE ARCHITECTURAL PARADIGM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AetherScribe implements a "Triad of Authority" architecture that strictly 
separates three critical system responsibilities:

A) DECISION AUTHORITY (Quantum Reinforcement Learning Layer)
   - Primary Component: Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC)
   - Library: PennyLane (Hybrid Quantum-Classical Framework)
   - Function: Resolves narrative branching decisions at the quantum level
   - Output: Probability distributions over possible story actions
   - Key Feature: Decisions are made BEFORE any text generation occurs

B) RENDERING AUTHORITY (Large Language Model Layer)
   - Primary Component: Google Gemini 2.0 Flash
   - Function: Converts quantum-resolved actions into coherent narrative prose
   - Constraint: Must adhere strictly to quantum-resolved trajectory
   - Output: Human-readable story text with stylistic consistency

C) EXPLAINABILITY AUTHORITY (Interpretation Layer)
   - Primary Component: Explanation Engine
   - Function: Translates quantum decision traces into human-understandable logic
   - Output: Narrative rationale, counterfactual analysis, influence attribution
   - Key Feature: Converts technical quantum mechanics into "plain English"

1.2 DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The separation of authorities ensures:
- Technical Transparency: Every decision is traceable to quantum mechanics
- Narrative Integrity: LLM cannot override quantum policy decisions
- Research Reproducibility: Quantum policy can be analyzed independently
- Explainability: Human stakeholders understand "why" and "why not" for actions

SECTION 2: QUANTUM REINFORCEMENT LEARNING CORE

2.1 QUANTUM CIRCUIT ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: QuantumPolicy class (quantum_policy/quantum_policy.py)

A) Quantum Device Configuration
   - Device Type: default.mixed (PennyLane)
   - Number of Qubits: 4 (configurable)
   - Quantum State: Mixed state representation for noise modeling

B) Circuit Structure (Three-Layer Design)
   Layer 1: State Encoding
   - Purpose: Encode narrative features into quantum state
   - Gate Type: RY (Rotation around Y-axis)
   - Input: Normalized state vector [Tension, Phase, Progress, Genre]
   - Effect: Maps classical narrative state to quantum superposition

   Layer 2: Character Trait Biasing
   - Purpose: Incorporate agent personalities into decision-making
   - Gate Type: RY (Rotation around Y-axis)
   - Input: Character trait vector (Aggression, Loyalty, Strategy, etc.)
   - Effect: "Pulls" probability distribution toward trait-aligned actions
   - Scale: Trait values × π (full rotation range)

   Layer 3: Variational Learning
   - Purpose: Trainable parameters for policy optimization
   - Gate Type: RZ (Rotation around Z-axis)
   - Parameters: Learned weights (initially random, updated via RL)
   - Range: [0, 2π] with clipping to prevent gradient explosion

C) Measurement Strategy
   - Measurement Type: Probability measurement on all qubits
   - Output: Probability distribution over 2^n quantum states
   - Normalization: Probabilities summed and normalized to 1.0
   - Action Selection: Probabilistic sampling from distribution

2.2 STATE ENCODING MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: StateRepresentation class (environment/state_representation.py)

The system encodes 4 critical narrative features:

A) Tension (Qubit 0)
   - Range: [0.0, 1.0]
   - Interpretation: Current dramatic intensity
   - Impact: High tension → more aggressive action probabilities
   - Genre Modifier: Multiplied by genre-specific coefficients

B) Phase (Qubit 1)
   - Discrete States: {intro, rising, climax}
   - Encoding: 0.0 (intro), 0.5 (rising), 1.0 (climax)
   - Interpretation: Position in narrative arc
   - Impact: Climax increases high-stakes action probabilities

C) Progress (Qubit 2)
   - Range: [0.0, 1.0]
   - Calculation: current_step / max_steps
   - Interpretation: Proximity to narrative conclusion
   - Impact: Influences pacing and action urgency

D) Genre (Qubit 3)
   - Types: {mystery, thriller, drama, science_fiction}
   - Encoding: One-hot style numeric mapping
   - Interpretation: Domain-specific behavioral expectations
   - Impact: Modulates tension dynamics and action appropriateness

2.3 CHARACTER TRAIT BIASING SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each agent possesses a trait dictionary that influences quantum decisions:

A) Protagonist Traits
   - aggression: Tendency toward confrontational actions
   - curiosity: Propensity for investigative actions
   - resilience: Resistance to setbacks and determination
   - empathy: Consideration of other characters' perspectives

B) Antagonist Traits
   - hostility: Direct opposition to protagonist
   - strategic_depth: Complexity of planning
   - manipulation: Psychological warfare tendencies
   - ruthlessness: Willingness to cross ethical lines

C) Ally Traits
   - loyalty: Commitment to protagonist's goals
   - supportiveness: Active assistance behaviors
   - resourcefulness: Problem-solving capability
   - sacrifice: Willingness to endure personal cost

D) Trait Application in Quantum Circuit
   - Traits are normalized to [0, 1] range
   - Multiplied by π to create rotation angles
   - Applied as RY gates in Layer 2 of circuit
   - Create constructive/destructive interference patterns
   - Result: Actions aligned with traits have higher probability

2.4 REINFORCEMENT LEARNING UPDATE MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Learning Method: Policy Gradient (Simplified REINFORCE)

Update Rule:
   weights[i] += learning_rate × reward × (action_index / (n_qubits + 1))

Components:
   - Learning Rate: Typically 0.01 (configurable)
   - Reward Signal: Derived from LLM coherence scores (0.0 - 1.0)
   - Weight Clipping: Ensures weights stay in [0, 2π] range
   - Gradient Direction: Positive rewards increase weights proportionally

Reward Signal Sources:
   A) Environment Reward (Narrative Physics)
      - Tension dynamics: Actions that maintain dramatic flow
      - Relationship coherence: Actions consistent with character bonds
      - Genre appropriateness: Domain-specific action fitness

   B) LLM Coherence Score (Semantic Audit)
      - Logical consistency: Story follows causal chains
      - Character voice: Dialogue matches personalities
      - Narrative flow: Smooth transitions between scenes
      - Range: [0.0, 1.0] floating-point score

2.5 ADVANCED QUANTUM FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A) Feature Sensitivity Analysis
   Purpose: Identify which narrative features most influence decisions
   Method: Finite difference approximation
   Process:
      1. Compute baseline action probabilities
      2. Perturb each state feature by small delta (0.05)
      3. Compute perturbed probabilities
      4. Measure KL divergence between distributions
      5. Normalize to create sensitivity scores
   Output: Normalized sensitivity vector (sums to 1.0)
   Use Case: Explainability - "Tension was the most important factor"

B) Quantum Entropy Calculation
   Purpose: Measure decision uncertainty
   Formula: H(P) = -Σ p_i × log₂(p_i)
   Range: [0, n_qubits] bits
   Interpretation:
      - Low entropy (< 1 bit): Deterministic decision, clear winner
      - High entropy (> 3 bits): High uncertainty, multiple viable paths
   Use Case: Narrative risk assessment, creativity metrics

C) Narrative Risk Estimation
   Purpose: Predict potential for story incoherence
   Formula: Risk = 0.6 × (Entropy / n_qubits) + 0.4 × Tension
   Components:
      - Entropy contribution: Uncertainty in decision-making
      - Tension contribution: Dramatic pressure
   Range: [0.0, 1.0]
   Use Case: Early warning system for narrative quality degradation

D) Influence Attribution
   Purpose: Determine which character "drives" the decision
   Method: Perturbation analysis on trait vectors
   Process:
      1. Compute baseline probabilities with all traits
      2. For each character, perturb their traits by +0.1
      3. Measure change in probability distribution
      4. Normalize changes to create attribution scores
   Output: {"Protagonist": 0.45, "Antagonist": 0.30, "Ally": 0.25}
   Use Case: Narrative agency tracking, character arc analysis

4.1 STORY ENVIRONMENT PHYSICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: StoryEnvironment class (environment/story_environment.py)

A) State Variables
   - Current Phase: {intro, rising, climax}
   - Tension Level: [0.0, 1.0] continuous
   - Step Counter: Integer progress tracker
   - Relationship Matrix: Multi-dimensional character bonds
   - Genre Context: Domain-specific rules

B) Phase Progression Logic
   Transition Rules:
      - intro → rising: At 33% of max_steps
      - rising → climax: At 66% of max_steps
      - climax → terminal: At 100% of max_steps
   
   Automatic Tension Modulation:
      - intro: Baseline tension ≈ 0.3
      - rising: Gradual increase toward 0.6
      - climax: Rapid escalation toward 0.8-1.0

C) Genre-Specific Dynamics
   Genre Tension Multipliers (genre_dynamics.py):
      - thriller: 1.5× (rapid escalation)
      - mystery: 0.8× (controlled suspense)
      - drama: 1.0× (standard pacing)
      - science_fiction: 1.2× (moderate intensity)

   Application:
      final_tension = base_tension × genre_multiplier × action_impact

4.2 RELATIONSHIP DYNAMICS SYSTEM (v5.4.2 Feature)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three relationship pairs with dual dimensions:

A) Protagonist-Ally Relationship
   Dimensions:
      - trust: [0.0, 1.0] - Confidence in ally's reliability
      - enmity: [0.0, 1.0] - Friction or disagreement
   
   Evolution Rules:
      ▲ trust when:
         - ally provides_critical_intelligence (+0.1)
         - ally sacrifice_personal_safety (+0.2)
      ▼ trust when:
         - ally fails to support in crisis (-0.1)
   
   Impact on Story:
      - High trust → More cooperative joint actions
      - Low trust → Protagonist isolation

B) Protagonist-Antagonist Relationship
   Dimensions:
      - trust: [0.0, 1.0] - Rare, only in truce scenarios
      - enmity: [0.0, 1.0] - Opposition intensity
   
   Evolution Rules:
      ▲ enmity when:
         - protagonist confront_adversary_directly (+0.15)
         - antagonist initiate_decisive_strike (+0.15)
         - antagonist deploy_psychological_manipulation (+0.10)
      ▼ enmity when:
         - protagonist negotiate_high_stakes_truce (-0.20)
   
   Impact on Story:
      - High enmity → Increased tension baseline
      - Moderate enmity → Strategic maneuvering

C) Ally-Antagonist Relationship
   Dimensions:
      - trust: [0.0, 1.0] - Usually zero
      - enmity: [0.0, 1.0] - Indirect opposition
   
   Evolution Rules:
      - Typically remains constant at moderate enmity (0.5)
      - Can increase if ally directly confronts antagonist

D) Friction Contribution to Tension
   Formula:
      friction = (P-A enmity + P-L enmity) × 0.05
      final_tension = base_tension + friction
   
   Effect: Interpersonal conflict adds persistent tension

4.3 REWARD SIGNAL GENERATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Composite Reward Function:

   total_reward = environment_reward + coherence_reward

A) Environment Reward Components
   1. Tension Maintenance (-1 to +1):
      - Reward maintaining tension in [0.4, 0.7] sweet spot
      - Penalize extreme low (<0.2) or high (>0.9) tension
   
   2. Phase Appropriateness (-0.5 to +0.5):
      - Reward actions matching current phase
      - Example: High-stakes actions rewarded in climax
   
   3. Relationship Coherence (-0.3 to +0.3):
      - Reward actions consistent with relationship states
      - Example: Penalize protagonist trusting antagonist

B) LLM Coherence Reward (0.0 to 1.0)
   Generated by StoryRenderer semantic audit:
   - Logical consistency: 30% weight
   - Character voice fidelity: 30% weight
   - Narrative flow smoothness: 40% weight
   
   Critique Protocol:
      1. LLM receives generated scene + quantum action
      2. Evaluates adherence to decisions
      3. Checks character name usage
      4. Assesses causal logic
      5. Returns single floating-point score

C) Reward Scaling and Normalization
   Final reward = 0.4 × env_reward + 0.6 × coherence_reward
   Range: [-0.4, 1.0]
   Use: Directly fed to policy gradient update

5.1 EXPLANATION GENERATION SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: ExplanationEngine class (explainability/explanation_engine.py)

A) Design Philosophy: Ultra-Simplified Language
   - Target Audience: Non-experts, stakeholders without ML background
   - Language Level: Child-appropriate explanations
   - Terminology: Avoids "quantum", "probability", "weights"
   - Character Focus: Uses actual character names, not role labels

B) Explanation Structure
   1. Decision Summary
      - What happened in plain terms
      - Which characters took which actions
      - Example: "Emma chose to investigate while Victor attacked"

   2. Rejection Analysis (Why-Not Logic)
      - Top 2 rejected action combinations
      - Simple reason for each rejection
      - Categories:
         • "Weak Potential" - Very low probability (<0.05)
         • "Logic Conflict" - Contradicts character motivations
         • "Character Mismatch" - Traits don't align
         • "Alternative Path" - Strong but not strongest
   
   3. Influence Attribution
      - Identifies the "main driver" of the decision
      - Links to character's dominant trait
      - Example: "Victor was the leader because he is very aggressive"
   
   4. Emotional Context
      - Translates tension to emotional language:
         • Tension > 0.7: "Very exciting and fast"
         • Tension < 0.3: "Calm and quiet"
         • Tension 0.3-0.7: "Getting more interesting"
   
   5. Conclusion
      - Simple statement affirming decision quality
      - Example: "This choice makes the most sense for the story"

5.2 COUNTERFACTUAL ANALYSIS (Why-Not Logic)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Process: Systematic rejection reason assignment

A) Probability-Based Classification
   Input: All non-chosen actions with their probabilities
   
   Thresholds and Reasons:
      if prob < 0.05:
         reason = "Weak Potential (too unlikely to pursue)"
      elif prob < max_prob × 0.3:
         reason = "Logic Conflict (contradicted character motivations)"
      elif prob < max_prob × 0.7:
         reason = "Character Mismatch (traits didn't align)"
      else:
         reason = "Alternative Path (strong but not strongest)"

B) LLM-Enhanced Rejection Analysis
   Component: StoryRenderer.analyze_rejections()
   
   Process:
      1. For each rejected action, create comparison prompt
      2. Include chosen action vs. rejected action
      3. Include quantum-generated rationale
      4. Request one-sentence simple explanation
      5. Store LLM-generated narrative reason
   
   Prompt Template:
      """
      DOMAIN: {genre}
      PATH WE USED: {chosen_action}
      PATH WE SKIPPED: {rejected_action}
      REASON: {technical_reason}
      
      Explain in ONE simple sentence why the skipped path was not as good.
      Use VERY EASY ENGLISH that a child can understand.
      Use character names ONLY.
      """

C) Output Format
   rejection_data = [
      {
         "index": 5,
         "action": {"Protagonist": "negotiate", "Antagonist": "strike"},
         "prob": 0.18,
         "reason": "Character Mismatch (Victor's aggressive nature..."
      },
      ...
   ]

6.1 STORY RENDERER ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: StoryRenderer class (llm/story_renderer.py)

A) LLM Configuration
   - Model: Google Gemini 2.0 Flash
   - API: google.generativeai Python SDK
   - Temperature: 0.8 (configurable, balance creativity/coherence)
   - Max Tokens: 1000 per scene generation

B) Three-Pass Rendering Protocol

   PASS 1: Scene Generation (Decision-First Protocol)
   Input:
      - Current narrative state
      - Quantum-resolved joint action
      - Rationale from ExplanationEngine
      - Previous scene summary (continuity)
      - Language preference
      - Mode (Short Story, Script, Novel)
   
   Prompt Structure:
      """
      [NARRATIVE RENDERER v5.4.2]
      DOMAIN: {genre}
      PHASE: {phase}
      CHARACTERS: {character_names_and_roles}
      
      PRIOR EVENT: {previous_summary}
      
      QUANTUM DECISION (IMMUTABLE):
      {character_1} must perform: {action_1}
      {character_2} must perform: {action_2}
      
      RATIONALE: {quantum_explanation}
      
      CONSTRAINTS:
      - Render the EXACT actions specified
      - Maintain character voice and personality
      - Ensure logical flow from previous event
      - Use {language} language
      - Format as {mode}
      - Target length: 300-400 words
      """
   
   Output: Raw narrative prose (300-1000 words)

   PASS 2: Summary Generation
   Input: Full scene text from Pass 1
   
   Prompt:
      "Summarize the following narrative in one concise sentence: {scene_text}"
   
   Output: Single-sentence summary for next scene's continuity
   Purpose: Compress information for context management

   PASS 3: Semantic Audit (Coherence Scoring)
   Input: Generated scene + quantum decision + previous state
   
   Prompt:
      """
      [NARRATIVE AUDIT PROTOCOL v5.4.2]
      Analyze for:
      1. Adherence to quantum decisions: {actions}
      2. Correct character name usage
      3. Logical consistency with previous state
      
      Output ONLY a floating-point number [0.0, 1.0]:
      Narrative Coherence Score
      """
   
   Output: Single float extracted via regex
   Fallback: 0.9 if extraction fails
   Use: Becomes coherence_reward in RL update

C) Prompt Template System
   Component: prompt_templates.py
   
   Features:
   - Genre-specific instructions
   - Phase-aware pacing guidelines
   - Character personality injection
   - Language-specific formatting
   - Mode-specific structure (prose vs. script)

6.2 NAME SYNCHRONIZATION SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem: LLM tends to invent names or use role labels

Solution: Absolute Name Enforcement

A) Director Brief Integration
   At initialization, user provides:
   director_brief = {
      "characters": {
         "protagonist": "Emma Chen",
         "antagonist": "Victor Kane",
         "ally": "Dr. Sarah Park"
      },
      "premise": "A cybersecurity expert discovers...",
      "setting": "Near-future Tokyo"
   }

B) Name Mapping Creation
   name_map = {
      "Protagonist": "Emma Chen",
      "Antagonist": "Victor Kane",
      "Ally": "Dr. Sarah Park"
   }

C) Prompt Injection
   Every LLM prompt includes:
   """
   CHARACTER IDENTITY (ABSOLUTE):
   - The Protagonist is named "Emma Chen" - NEVER call them "The Protagonist"
   - The Antagonist is named "Victor Kane" - NEVER call them "The Antagonist"
   - The Ally is named "Dr. Sarah Park" - NEVER call them "The Ally"
   """

D) Post-Generation Validation
   - Regex scan for role labels ("Protagonist", "Antagonist", "Ally")
   - If found, reduce coherence_score by 0.1 per violation
   - Optionally trigger regeneration if violations > 2

E) Explanation Engine Integration
   - All explanations use name_map
   - Replace role with actual name in all outputs
   - Example: "Victor (Antagonist)" → "Victor"

7.1 EPISODE RUNNER ORCHESTRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Component: EpisodeRunner class (evaluation/episode_runner.py)

A) Execution Flow
   Initialization:
      - Load environment, policy, action space
      - Initialize explainer and renderer
      - Set hyperparameters (lr, epsilon, temperature)
   
   Main Loop (until max_steps or terminal state):
      Step 1: State Encoding
         - Extract narrative features
         - Create 4D state vector
      
      Step 2: Quantum Superposition
         - Combine character traits
         - Compute action probabilities
         - Calculate entropy, risk, attribution
      
      Step 3: Measurement (Collapse)
         - Sample action from probability distribution
         - Map index to joint action dictionary
      
      Step 4: Governance (Why-Not Analysis)
         - Collect all non-chosen actions
         - Assign rejection reasons
         - Sort by probability (descending)
      
      Step 5: Environment Transition
         - Apply joint action to environment
         - Update tension, phase, relationships
         - Receive environment reward
         - Check terminal condition
      
      Step 6: Rationale Generation
         - Compute quantum expectations
         - Pass to ExplanationEngine
         - Generate simple-language explanation
      
      Step 7: LLM Rendering
         - Call StoryRenderer (3-pass protocol)
         - Receive scene text, summary, coherence score
      
      Step 8: Reward Composition
         - Combine environment and coherence rewards
         - Update quantum policy weights
      
      Step 9: Logging
         - Store all telemetry in episode_log
         - Save state, action, rewards, explanations
   
   Return:
      - Full episode log (list of step dictionaries)
      - Total reward
      - Final narrative state

B) Telemetry Capture
   Each step records:

8.4 USE CASE: EXPLAINABILITY VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Research Question: Do explanations match human intuition?

Experimental Design:
   1. Generate 20 decision points
   2. Collect system-generated explanations
   3. Present to human evaluators (non-experts)
   4. Survey questions:
      - Is the explanation understandable? (1-5 Likert)
      - Does it match your intuition? (yes/no)
      - Does it help you trust the decision? (1-5 Likert)
   
   Success Criteria:
      - Average understandability > 4.0
      - Intuition match rate > 75%
      - Trust score > 3.5

8.5 USE CASE: COUNTERFACTUAL EXPLORATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Research Question: What would happen if different actions were chosen?

Experimental Design:
   1. Run episode to completion, save trajectory
   2. Select critical decision point (e.g., Step 3)
   3. Force alternative action (second-highest probability)
   4. Continue episode from that divergence
   5. Compare:
      - Narrative coherence (original vs. counterfactual)
      - Tension trajectory
      - Character relationship evolution
      - Final reward
   
   Insight:
      - Quantify "cost" of suboptimal decisions
      - Validate rejection reasons
      - Explore narrative multiverse

9.2 COMPARISON TO PRIOR WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Traditional RL Narrative Systems:
   - Policy: Neural network (black box)
   - Explainability: Limited to attention visualization
   - Character modeling: Explicit rule-based systems
   - LLM role: Post-hoc text generation only

AetherScribe Quantum System:
   - Policy: Variational quantum circuit (interpretable)
   - Explainability: Full decision trace + why-not analysis
   - Character modeling: Emergent from quantum interference
   - LLM role: Active participant in reward loop

Advantage:
   - Quantum entropy provides creativity metric (not possible with NN)
   - Trait biasing more parameter-efficient than large embedding layers
   - Separation of decision/rendering enables rigorous evaluation

9.3 LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Current Limitations:
   1. Quantum Circuit Scale
      - 4 qubits limits state representation granularity
      - Cannot encode complex world states (object locations, etc.)
      - Future: Extend to 6-8 qubits for richer features
   
   2. Action Space Combinatorics
      - 72 joint actions is manageable but constraining
      - Cannot model more than 3 agents
      - Future: Hierarchical action decomposition
   
   3. LLM Dependency
      - Requires API access (cost, latency)
      - Coherence scoring is approximate
      - Future: Local LLM deployment, fine-tuned reward model
   
   4. Lack of Long-Term Memory
      - No persistent world state beyond relationships
      - Cannot reference events from >2 steps ago
      - Future: Integrate external memory (vector database)
   
   5. Genre Limitations
      - Only 4 predefined genres
      - No mixed-genre support
      - Future: Continuous genre embedding space

Research Directions:
   - Quantum advantage benchmarks (vs. classical RL)
   - Human-in-the-loop policy refinement
   - Multi-modal narratives (text + image generation)
   - Real-time interactive storytelling
   - Cross-lingual narrative generation

10.4 PARAMETER TUNING GUIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Learning Rate (lr):
   - Default: 0.01
   - High (0.05-0.1): Fast adaptation, unstable
   - Low (0.001-0.005): Slow learning, stable
   - Recommendation: Start at 0.01, increase if rewards plateau

Temperature (LLM):
   - Default: 0.8
   - High (1.0-1.5): More creative, less predictable
   - Low (0.3-0.5): More deterministic, safer
   - Recommendation: 0.8 for balanced creativity

Epsilon (Quantum Noise):
   - Default: 0.0 (no noise)
   - Small (0.01-0.05): Adds exploration
   - Large (0.1+): Degrades coherence
   - Recommendation: 0.0 unless studying robustness

Max Steps:
   - Default: 5
   - Short (3-4): Quick experiments, incomplete arcs
   - Medium (5-7): Standard story length
   - Long (10+): Epic narratives, computational cost
   - Recommendation: 5 for testing, 7-10 for quality stories

SECTION 13: GLOSSARY OF TERMS
================================================================================

Action Space: Set of all possible actions agents can take
Attribution: Measure of which character drives a decision
Coherence Score: LLM-evaluated narrative quality (0.0-1.0)
Counterfactual: "What-if" alternative trajectory analysis
Entropy: Measure of decision uncertainty (bits)
Episode: Complete narrative from start to terminal state
Genre Multiplier: Coefficient scaling tension by story type
Interference: Quantum effect where trait biases combine
Joint Action: Simultaneous actions from all agents
Measurement: Quantum collapse to specific action
Phase: Story arc position (intro/rising/climax)
Policy: Mapping from state to action probabilities
Quantum Circuit: PennyLane circuit implementing policy
Qubit: Quantum bit, basic unit of quantum information
Rationale: Explanation for why action was chosen
Rejection: Action with probability but not chosen
Renderer: LLM component generating story text
Risk Score: Predicted probability of incoherence
Sensitivity: How much each state feature influences decision
State Vector: Numerical encoding of narrative state
Superposition: Quantum state before measurement
Telemetry: Logged metrics and diagnostics
Tension: Dramatic intensity (0.0-1.0)
Trait Vector: Numerical encoding of character personality
VQC: Variational Quantum Circuit
